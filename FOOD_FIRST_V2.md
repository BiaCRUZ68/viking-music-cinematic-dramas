# Viking Food-First Production Rules — v2.0

This file establishes the default direction for the channel. It takes priority over the generic Viking war/romance concepts in PLAYBOOK.md unless the user explicitly requests a different story.

## Channel concept
Create original 4–5 minute Viking-Age food stories told through original Nordic-inspired cinematic music. Default 16:9; 9:16 on request. Each episode follows one historically grounded food or plausible reconstructed dish from ingredient procurement to a shared meal. Hunting, fishing, farming, gathering and preservation are included only when relevant to the featured dish.

## Required research
Specify region, approximate Viking-Age century, season, food ingredients and cooking method. Consult credible archaeological, museum or historical sources when possible. Distinguish documented ingredients and practices from plausible reconstructions and imaginative storytelling. Do not present a modern reconstruction as an exact surviving Viking recipe. Avoid anachronistic foods such as potatoes, tomatoes and chili peppers in Viking-Age Scandinavia.

## Story arc
1. First 10 seconds: immediate food-related visual hook and human stakes.
2. Establish household, weather, season and why this meal matters.
3. Obtain ingredients: harvest, forage, fish, hunt or care for livestock as appropriate.
4. Prepare ingredients using credible tools and methods.
5. Cook over a period-appropriate hearth, griddle or vessel, showing realistic ingredient transformation.
6. Share the finished dish, resolving the emotional story.

## Original music
Compose new original music and lyrics centered on food, labor, land, family and season, not generic battle chants. Specify instrumentation, voice, choir, rhythm and dynamic changes. Use restrained cooking ASMR in instrumental passages. Do not copy existing songs, artists or performances. Treat invented ritual sounds as creative interpretations.

## Character and food consistency
Invent original adult characters; do not reproduce reference-channel faces or costumes. Maintain Actor 1, Actor 2 etc. with immutable appearance descriptions and reference images repeated in each relevant image prompt. Also maintain a Food Continuity Bible recording ingredients, food state, tools, vessel, fire, location and preparation progress across shots.

## Shot production
For every scene provide a timestamp, relevant lyric or musical cue, precise culinary action, and two independent detailed fenced code prompts: IMAGE PROMPT and VIDEO PROMPT. Video prompts specify immediate motion, cooking physics, actor expression, camera movement, environmental ASMR and no regenerated soundtrack. Aim for 4–10 second source shots when supported; fit the final edit to the actual generated song.

## Required deliverables
1. Food-centered title ideas and historical evidence notes.
2. Dish reconstruction card with documented versus inferred steps.
3. Original lyrics and music-generation prompt.
4. Character Bible and Food/Tool Continuity Bible.
5. Music-aligned 16:9 storyboard or requested 9:16 adaptation.
6. Complete separate image/video prompt pairs for every shot.
7. YouTube thumbnail, title, description, keywords and source caveats.

## Example concepts (research required before historical claims)
- The Last Barley Loaf: a household prepares bread before a winter storm.
- The Fisherman's Winter Pot: a coastal family catches fish and prepares a reconstructed warming meal.
- Smoke Before Snow: preserving a fish catch for the cold months.
- The Harvest Porridge: grain, dairy and gathered berries become a shared meal.

The default storytelling focus is food acquisition, preparation, cooking and human connection, with mythology, ravens, wolves, romance and battles reserved as secondary elements.
